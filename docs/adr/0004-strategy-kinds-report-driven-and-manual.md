# A Strategy is either report-driven or manual, told apart by a stored kind

A Strategy is the umbrella for a named bucket of listed-asset money, and it comes in two kinds. A **Report Strategy** is fed by an Edition and planned through Targets. A **Manual Strategy** — the legacy bucket — has no Edition and no Targets, at most one per Asset Class; it counts in its class Actual through Attribution, generates no plan orders, is ring-fenced from Funding, and its units leave only through an Attribution Transfer or a manual sell. The kind is a persisted column, never inferred from the absence of an Edition, because a Report Strategy is edition-less between its creation and its first import — inferring "manual" there would silently re-home a strategy that is merely waiting for its report.

The Manual Strategy is a first-class Strategy rather than a separate concept so the catalog, the class sums and the attribution ledger keep one vocabulary. It needs no synthetic Target to exist, which is what keeps the target chain meaning "what the reports plan".

**Considered options**: a separate Legacy Holding entity (two concepts for one catalog, duplicated grouping and attribution rules, and every class sum becomes a union of two lists); a synthetic Target for the bucket (corrupts the target chain, breaks the per-class sum, and makes the plan propose orders for money that is meant to sit still); a per-Position disposition field (a second state to keep in sync with attribution, and it cannot express a ticker split between the bucket and a report slice).

The decision is hard to unwind: the kind is persisted and read by planning, funding and every strategy screen, and the existing rows backfill as report-driven. Removing it means re-deriving the kind from data that deliberately does not carry it — exactly the ambiguity the column removes. Converting a Strategy between kinds is out of scope, so any future conversion has to be designed as its own act rather than as a side effect of editing a field.
